# Module 05 — Introduction to Indicators

## L01 — What Makes an Indicator Useful
**Outcome:** Explain how indicators operationalise results and distinguish indicators from targets. **Bloom:** Understand. **Time:** 50 min.

An indicator is a quantitative or qualitative variable used to measure progress or change. A target is the intended value of that indicator by a specified time. 'Employment improved' is neither an indicator nor a target. 'Percentage of graduates employed six months after completion' is an indicator. 'Increase from 42% to 60% by December 2027' adds baseline, target and time.

Useful indicators are decision-relevant, valid, sufficiently reliable, feasible to collect, sensitive to meaningful change and interpretable when disaggregated.

## L02 — Indicator Types and SMART/CREAM Tests
**Outcome:** Apply quality criteria to output, outcome and impact indicators. **Bloom:** Apply. **Time:** 60 min.

Indicators can measure quantity, quality, timeliness, coverage, equity, efficiency, behaviour or condition. They may be counts, proportions, rates, ratios, scores or qualitative ratings.

Use **SMART** as a practical test: Specific, Measurable, Achievable/appropriate, Relevant, Time-bound where the statement includes a target. Use **CREAM** as a complementary test: Clear, Relevant, Economic, Adequate, Monitorable.

### Guided exercise
Critique: `Number of youth empowered`; `Quality of training improved`; `80% employment`; `Percentage of enrolled women completing accredited training`. Rewrite weak indicators and explain exactly what improved.

## L03 — Design Indicators from a Results Framework
**Outcome:** Construct a balanced indicator set and explain its measurement logic. **Bloom:** Analyse. **Time:** 90 min.

### Case lab
Using the Level 1 results chain/logframe, create at least 12 indicators: implementation/output, outcome, quality/equity and one longer-term measure. For percentage indicators define numerator and denominator. Specify unit, disaggregation, frequency, source and responsible role.

Use the synthetic dataset to calculate: enrolment count; training completion rate among enrolled participants; six-month employment rate among completers; results by sex and county. Do not interpret a subgroup difference as causal evidence.
