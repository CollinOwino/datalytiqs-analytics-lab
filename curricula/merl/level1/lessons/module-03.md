# Module 03 — Theory of Change

## L01 — Theory of Change Fundamentals
**Outcome:** Explain the purpose and components of a Theory of Change (ToC). **Bloom:** Understand. **Time:** 50 min.

A Theory of Change explains how and why an intervention is expected to contribute to desired change in a particular context. Unlike a list of activities, it exposes causal pathways, mechanisms, assumptions, contextual factors and alternative explanations.

A useful ToC contains: problem/context; target groups; interventions; intermediate changes; outcomes; impact; causal mechanisms; assumptions; risks; external factors; and evidence needs.

### Worked example
Training may improve employment only if skills match labour demand, employers recognise the credential, graduates can search for vacancies, and constraints such as transport or discrimination do not block access. Those conditions belong in the causal model.

## L02 — Assumptions, Risks and Causal Pathways
**Outcome:** Identify and prioritise assumptions and risks that can break a causal pathway. **Bloom:** Apply. **Time:** 60 min.

An **assumption** is a condition believed necessary for the causal pathway to hold. A **risk** is an uncertain event or condition that may undermine implementation or results. An **external factor** sits outside programme control but can influence outcomes.

### Guided exercise
For the pathway `training → improved skills → employment → improved livelihood`, identify at least two assumptions, one measurable risk and one external factor at every transition. Rate each assumption by importance and uncertainty. High-importance/high-uncertainty assumptions become priority learning questions.

## L03 — Build and Critique a Theory of Change
**Outcome:** Create and defend a testable Theory of Change. **Bloom:** Analyse. **Time:** 90 min.

### Case lab
Construct a ToC for the County Youth Employment Programme. Your diagram or structured narrative must include the problem, target groups, intervention components, short/intermediate/long-term outcomes, mechanisms, assumptions, risks and external influences.

Then critique it using five tests: **causal plausibility, evidence plausibility, contextual plausibility, measurability, and falsifiability**.

### Professional output
Submit (1) ToC diagram/narrative, (2) assumptions register, and (3) 250-word causal justification.

### Completion condition
At least three causal transitions are explicitly justified and at least one assumption is converted into a future monitoring/evaluation question.
