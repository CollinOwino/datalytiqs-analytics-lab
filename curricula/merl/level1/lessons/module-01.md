# Module 01 — Introduction to Monitoring, Evaluation and Learning

## L01 — What M&E and Learning Are
**Outcome:** Explain monitoring, evaluation and learning and identify the management question each serves. **Bloom:** Understand. **Time:** 45 min.

Monitoring is the continuous, systematic collection and use of information about implementation and results. It answers questions such as: Are activities occurring? Are outputs being delivered? Are targets being reached? Where is performance deviating from plan?

Evaluation is a systematic assessment of the design, implementation, results or value of an intervention. It normally goes beyond tracking to ask why results occurred, whether an intervention was relevant or effective, what unintended effects occurred, and what should change.

Learning is the deliberate conversion of evidence into improved decisions and practice. A programme can collect excellent data yet fail as a learning system if findings never alter budgets, targeting, delivery or strategy.

### Worked example
A youth-employment programme trains 1,000 young people. Monthly counts of enrolment and completion are monitoring. A study examining whether the training improved employment outcomes is evaluation. A management meeting that changes course content after evidence shows weak employer alignment is learning.

### Guided exercise
For each question, label it Monitoring, Evaluation or Learning and justify your choice: (1) How many trainees completed this quarter? (2) Did training improve employment prospects? (3) What should management change next quarter given the evidence?

## L02 — Monitoring vs Evaluation vs Research vs Audit
**Outcome:** Differentiate five evidence functions using purpose, timing and standards. **Bloom:** Apply. **Time:** 50 min.

Research seeks systematic knowledge and may or may not concern a specific programme decision. Audit tests compliance, controls, transactions or conformity against criteria. Inspection verifies condition or compliance, often at a point in time. Monitoring tracks implementation and results routinely. Evaluation judges an intervention using explicit questions, criteria and evidence.

The categories can overlap. A data-quality audit can support monitoring; evaluation can use research methods. The correct classification depends on the primary decision purpose.

### Case activity
The County Youth Employment Programme has: quarterly beneficiary reports; an annual financial compliance review; a university study of youth labour-market transitions; a mid-term assessment of effectiveness; and a management after-action review. Classify each and state what it can and cannot conclude.

### Common error
Calling every evidence activity 'M&E' hides different standards of proof. A routine dashboard cannot establish causal impact merely because it contains outcome indicators.
